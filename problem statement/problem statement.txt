## Background (all tasks)

You're working on the Retail Analytics and Strategy team at Quantium. Your client is the Category Manager for Chips at a supermarket chain. She wants a clearer picture of who buys chips and how they shop, so she can build a strategic plan for the chips category for the next six months. Later, she also wants to know whether a new store layout trial actually increased sales, so she can decide whether to roll it out to all stores.

---

## Task 1: Data Preparation and Customer Analytics

**Objective:** Clean and explore the transaction and customer datasets, then use them to understand chip-buying behavior across different customer segments.

**What you're given:**
- A transaction dataset covering roughly a year of purchases across stores
- A customer dataset with demographic info — life stage (e.g., young singles, families, retirees) and premium/budget/mainstream customer segment

**What to do:**
- Inspect both datasets for irregularities: missing values, negative or zero quantities, duplicate transactions, unusual product names, and outliers (e.g., a customer buying an implausibly large quantity in one transaction)
- Filter the transaction data down to chip-related products, cleaning up product names (stripping package sizes, special characters, etc.) so products can be grouped sensibly
- Remove or investigate non-chip items that might have slipped in (e.g., salsa, which sometimes appears in similar product categories)
- Merge the transaction and customer datasets together
- Analyze total sales, number of customers, and average spend broken down by life stage and customer segment
- Identify which segments drive the most chip sales and spending, and look at things like purchase frequency and pack size preferences across segments
- Summarize findings into a short set of insights and recommendations that could inform the Category Manager's strategic plan

**Deliverable:** A short written summary (or slide) of key insights, supported by charts/tables, aimed at a non-technical business stakeholder.

---

## Task 2: Experimentation and Uplift Testing

**Objective:** Determine whether a trial of a new store layout increased chip sales, using a rigorous store-to-store comparison.

**Context:** Three stores (referred to as trial stores) tested a new layout. You need to know if their sales performance improved *because of* the trial, not just due to normal variation.

**What to do:**
- Using the cleaned data from Task 1, calculate monthly sales metrics per store: total sales, number of customers, and transactions per customer
- For each trial store, select the best-matching "control" store from among all other stores — one whose historical sales pattern (pre-trial) most closely resembles the trial store's, so the comparison isn't biased
- Common approach: use a correlation or magnitude-distance metric between each candidate store's monthly sales trend and the trial store's trend to score similarity, then pick the highest-scoring match
- Once matched, compare the trial store's performance *during* the trial period against the scaled/control store's performance over the same period
- Assess whether the difference is statistically meaningful (not just random fluctuation) — this usually involves calculating a percentage difference and checking it against expected variation from the pre-trial period
- Repeat this process independently for each of the three trial stores
- Draw a conclusion for each store: did the trial layout lead to a measurable uplift in sales, customers, or transactions?

**Deliverable:** An analysis (with visualizations comparing trial vs. control store trends) and a clear recommendation on whether each trial layout appears to have been successful.

---

## Task 3: Analytics and Commercial Application

**Objective:** Package the findings from Tasks 1 and 2 into a polished, business-ready presentation for the Category Manager.

**What to do:**
- Consolidate the customer segment insights and the trial store results into a cohesive narrative
- Design clear, professional visuals (charts, tables) that support your recommendations — avoid clutter, make it easy for a non-technical stakeholder to skim and understand
- Structure the presentation logically: context/background → key findings on customer behavior → trial results → recommendations
- Make actionable recommendations: e.g., which customer segments to target, what layout changes seem worth rolling out further, and any caveats or next steps

**Deliverable:** A short slide deck or report presenting the findings and strategic recommendations, written for a business audience rather than a technical one.
